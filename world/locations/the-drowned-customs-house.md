## Public description

A stone customs house that the channel took nine years ago. The ground floor holds a metre of green water at low tide and three metres at high. The upper floors are dry, reachable by a rope through a river-side window, and officially sealed by Magistrate order.

## Immediate danger

The tide. A character inside the ground floor when the water turns has roughly twelve minutes before the stair is impassable. The stair rail is rotten and takes weight once.

## Useful details

The upper floor holds eleven years of confiscation ledgers, swollen but readable. The ledgers record every bronze seizure by date, weight, and seizing officer. Orso Pell's name appears against sixty-one entries, and thirty-eight of those seizures never reached the Magistrate strongroom.

A window on the river side gives a clear view of the bell tower and of anyone entering the road shrine path.

## Hidden truths

A Choir Below listener named Ammet keeps watch here in daylight, unarmed, with a glass and a signal lamp. He is a rope-maker with a family and he is not looking for a fight. He will talk if approached without weapons drawn, and he will trade the truth about Sera Vane for a promise to hear the Choir's terms.

The thirty-eight missing seizures went to the Choir. Ammet knows this and considers it a fair trade for charts.

## NPC motives

Ammet wants the party to carry a message into the river cave rather than a blade. He believes, correctly, that the negotiation is nearly finished and that armed strangers will collapse it.

## Discoverable clues

Reading the ledgers for ten minutes reveals the missing thirty-eight seizures. A search of the watch position finds a signal lamp with a Choir mark and a chart fragment showing a channel into the cliff that no Compact chart shows.

## Consequences

If the party takes the ledgers to the Magistrates, Orso Pell is arrested within a day and the Choir loses its bronze supply, which advances the Choir's clock by two segments. If the party kills Ammet, every Choir member in Vey learns their faces by evening.
