## Public description

A roadside shrine of black stone on the river bank, roofed and open on three sides. The shrine bell hangs at head height and shows a crack running a third of its height. A strip of open mud lies between the road and the shrine wall, and anyone crossing it is visible from the road for about twenty seconds.

## Immediate danger

Exposure. The road carries Magistrate patrols every second hour. Below the shrine, the bank drops four metres to fast water.

## Useful details

Sera Vane kept a keeper's box under the bench: a tide table, a striking hammer, and a ledger of every ringing for six years. The final entry is two nights ago and reads, in her hand, "It answered on the third stroke. Going down to hear the rest."

The crack in the bell is old and has been packed with lead, badly, at least twice.

## Hidden truths

A stair behind the shrine's rear panel descends to the Black Bell crypt. The panel is not locked, only heavy, and the shrine keepers have known about it for nine generations.

The bell answers. Struck three times after dark, it produces a fourth tone that nobody strikes. Sera Vane recorded this fourth tone eleven times before she went down.

## NPC motives

None present. The shrine is empty and that is the point: a bell-keeper's post is never empty.

## Discoverable clues

An active search under the bench finds the keeper's box without a roll. Striking the bell three times after dark produces the answering tone, which is the clearest signal in the adventure that the situation is not superstition. The rear panel yields to a deliberate search or to any character who thinks about where a crypt stair would be.

## Consequences

Striking the bell after dark alerts the listener in the customs house and advances the Choir's clock by one segment. Taking the ledger to Mother Keth opens her willingness to speak about the original terms.
