# The Black Estuary

A dying coast where the bronze bells that hold back the sea have begun to crack.

## The bargain

Nine generations ago the estuary towns cast bronze bells and hung them along the water. The bells were not decoration. Rung on the tide, they kept something in the deep water asleep and kept the salt out of the fields. The towns paid for the bronze with everything they had, and the arrangement held.

The bells are now old. Three have cracked in eleven years. Every crack widens the gap in the sound, and every gap lets a little more of the deep water's attention into the estuary. Nobody living remembers who negotiated the original terms. Everyone remembers who pays when the ringing stops.

## The Black Estuary today

The estuary drains a flooded delta between salt marsh and broken hills. Tides run four metres and move fast. Channels shift each season, so pilots are wealthy and charts are worthless within a year. Bronze is the only currency the old houses respect.

Vey is the starting settlement: a flooded customs town of perhaps two thousand people, governed badly by three rival houses. Half the ground floors are abandoned to the water. People live above the tide line and walk on planks.

## The starting situation

Sera Vane, the bell-keeper of the road shrine, has not rung the evening bell for two days. She told a fishmonger, before she vanished, that something beneath the old river shrine was answering the bells.

## Tone

Doomed sword and sorcery. Consequences are physical and near. Magic is a bargain with something that keeps the receipt. Nobody is coming to help. A character who survives a season is remarkable; a character who survives a year is a story people tell.

The world does not wait for the party. Clocks advance when the party stalls.
