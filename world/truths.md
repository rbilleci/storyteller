# Truths of the Black Estuary

These statements are true. The narrator may reveal them through play. The narrator must not contradict them.

## Public truths

Every person in Vey knows these. State them freely.

The bells hold back the sea and everyone believes it, whether or not they can explain how. Three bells have cracked in eleven years. The Salt Magistrates tax bronze at a punitive rate and nobody has successfully smuggled a bell casting out of the estuary. Tides run fast enough to drown a strong swimmer in the channel. The bell-keeper of the road shrine is Sera Vane, and she has not rung the evening bell for two days.

## Half-known truths

A knowledgeable character may learn these with time, contacts, or a successful test.

The Choir Below is not a rumour: it is an organised faith with perhaps forty adherents in Vey, and its members hold ordinary jobs. The Ferrymen's Compact keeps the only accurate channel charts and updates them monthly. Orso Pell, the customs captain, has been quietly buying cracked bronze for six years and nobody has asked why.

## Hidden truths

Never state these to players without discovery in fiction. The `scene_commit` tool records them as hidden facts when the party learns them.

The thing beneath the river cave is not asleep and has not been asleep for two years. It answers bells because sound is the only language the original bargain left it. It wants the bargain renegotiated, not broken, and it will deal.

Sera Vane is alive in the river cave. She went willingly. She is negotiating, badly, on behalf of a town that did not ask her to.

Orso Pell buys cracked bronze because the Choir Below pays him in charts, and the charts show a channel that should not exist. He does not know what made it.

The Black Bell in the crypt is uncracked and unhung. Hanging it would restore the sound and end the negotiation permanently, on the deep water's original terms, which nobody alive has read.

Mother Keth read the original terms as a girl. She went blind two days later. She has never told anyone what the terms say, and she will not, unless someone she trusts is about to hang the Black Bell.
